Phase 1 judge report

What was built, what it scores, what it costs, and why. The judge lets a 1.7B drafter pre-fill each 32-token block so the 30B commits more tokens per pass. Headline: at 128M training tokens and without DES48 expert pooling, the judge matches stock SDAR-30B within noise on all seven benchmarks while cutting MoE passes per block by 1.30–1.41×.

  • Pooling does not change the number of passes; it caps experts at 48 per layer-forward. Turning it off costs compute (net 1.28–1.42× → 1.07–1.18×) and ~10% wall-clock, and recovers GPQA (+6.6) and IFEval (+12.9).
  • The drafter is ~3–4 ms per token, about 4% of the pipeline.
  • Against an autoregressive decode (48 layers × 8 experts = 384 expert loads per token) block-32 diffusion still moves more expert weight per token; an AR baseline on this harness is the open comparison.

Why GPQA and IFEval dropped with DES48

  • Not forgetting: base weights and routers are frozen; the LoRA is active only at draft-filled slots inside judge passes. Teacher-forced on stock's own GPQA answers, the 128M judge predicts the 30B slightly better than 28.5M on every slice (acc .486 → .492, override precision .845 → .860).
  • Not overtraining or a data shift: science data between 28.5M and 128M had the same sources, subjects, MC share and answer lengths; only its volume grew (6×). Held-out probe improves.
  • DES48 pooling was the cause: removing it restores GPQA 30.3 → 36.9 (stock 37.4) and IFEval 38.1 → 51.0 (stock 52.7). Pooling on the KV-flush pass stores the committed text from a restricted expert set; errors compound over long reasoning.
  • Answer extraction for GPQA (_gpqa_letter, code/analyze_s1b.py) misses "answer is choice X" and "**X. …**" and once mis-reads a variable; lenient re-scoring shifts runs by 1–3 questions.
