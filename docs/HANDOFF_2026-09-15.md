## 1. What the project is

Speed up decoding of the masked-diffusion MoE `JetLM/SDAR-30B-A3B-Chat-b32` (128 experts, block 32) with minimal
accuracy loss versus the released ("stock"/baseline) model. Mechanism: a small dense sibling (`JetLM/SDAR-1.7B-Chat-b32`,
unchanged) drafts the whole block first — the *general expert* — and a LoRA *judge* (27M params, r32/α64 on q,k,v,o +
a draft-flag embedding, router gates frozen) on the 30B reads the filled block and commits the tokens it trusts
(confirm the draft where it agrees, override where not). Decode-time DES expert pooling (48 of 128 experts per forward)
cuts experts per token. Decoder: `code/draftalign/run_da_decode.py --judge full --draft s8 [--pool 48 --pool-rule des --pool-flush]`.
Metrics (AGENTS.md §8): accuracy, paired Δ vs stock with 2,000-resample bootstrap CI, passes/block, experts/token,
speedup = stock experts/token ÷ arm's (the drafter is not charged).

Interpretation: the calibrated judge supplies the accuracy recovery (HumanEval 75.0 vs 69.5 for the uncalibrated
epoch-2 judge at the same ~1.5×; confident commits >99% precise, holdout ECE 0.008); the DES48 pool supplies most of
the speedup (the same judge without it gives 1.05–1.22×); on GPQA the plain judge matches stock while the pooled
variants lose 8–10 points, so that gap comes from pooling on science multiple-choice, not from the judge.
Training-side: r3_B's train accuracy equals r3_A's, its overconfidence gap is ~0 (r3_A: +0.05 and growing), it commits
fewer slots per pass (34% vs 40% above 0.95), override slots stay slightly overconfident (+0.09, watch item).
