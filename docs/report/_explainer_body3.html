Building the missing verifier

The plan is to teach the model, and only the model, one new behaviour: at a slot that holds a tentative draft token, output the token that actually belongs there rather than a copy of the next input.

What is trained

Nothing large. The experts, the embeddings, the output head and the drafter are all frozen. The trainable set is a rank-32 LoRA adapter on the four attention projections of all 48 layers, plus one vector:

LoRA writes W x + (α/r)·B A x with thin trainable A and B, and B initialised to zero, so training starts at exactly the stock model. Attention is the right place for this update: "copy the token at my own slot" is an identity attention pattern and "infer a slot from its neighbours" is the masked-slot machinery SDAR already has. The adapter's job is to change which of the two fires at a filled slot — a change in where attention looks, which is precisely what a low-rank update to q, k, v, o can express.

What one training example is

One example is an (item, block) pair, and its state is re-randomised every time it is drawn. This corruption process is the heart of the method, because it must reproduce the states the judge will actually meet:

  • with probability 0.4, nothing is committed — the pass-1 state;
  • otherwise, a recorded denoising pass is drawn and its committed set is used;
  • each still-open slot then holds the drafter's token with probability 1−m and [MASK] with probability m, with m uniform on [0,1];
  • with probability 0.3, m = 0 exactly — every open slot draft-filled, the literal pass-1 inference state.

Crucially, the corruption is the real drafter's own errors, not uniform noise. Prior work on correcting filled tokens in diffusion models corrupts with random or self-sampled tokens; here the wrong tokens the judge must learn to catch are exactly the wrong tokens it will meet.

The targets, and the loss

The target at every open slot is the token that block ended up with in the big model's own decode — specifically, the free rule's decode from section 5, keeping only items whose final answer was correct. Quality is protected by the targets being the model's own outputs, not by any exactness argument. Where the draft was right, the target equals it (a confirmation); where it was wrong, the target differs (an override).

L = meani∈O [ −log pi(Ti) ] cross-entropy, all open slots + β · meani∈C H( softmax(zi / τ) ) sharpen, only where already right O = open slots, C = { i ∈ O : argmax pi = Ti } β = 2.0, τ = 0.5, the [MASK] column removed before H

The first term teaches what to say. The second is dParallel's certainty forcing: where the model's top guess is already the target, push its probability toward 1 so the slot clears the 0.95 commit threshold in one pass instead of three. It is never applied to a wrong slot, so it sharpens a correct distribution rather than entrenching a wrong one. The [MASK] column is removed because entropy could otherwise be minimised by piling probability on a token that is never a legal output.

Not in the loss: the drafter; any pull toward the original model; committed slots; prefix tokens.

The budget, and its honest smallness

Round 1 and round 2a both trained on 3,000 GSM8K prompts → 2,843 correct → 16,905 blocks (16,334 train / 571 held out, split by item so no block of a held-out item is ever trained on). Two epochs at a global batch of 32 states is 1,057 optimiser steps, AdamW at 2e−5, cosine decay, ~5.3 s per step on 8 GPUs. With ~20 open slots per block that is roughly 600,000 supervised slots — 7 to 15 times less than dParallel used for a comparable behaviour change. Under-confidence was flagged in advance as the first thing to go wrong, and it is exactly what went wrong.

Round 1 failed, and the failure was informative

The first adapter had no draft flag and no gate. It was worse than doing nothing.

The diagnostic is a single comparison. At draft-filled slots, round 1's held-out accuracy reached 0.393. The trivial policy "just copy the input token" scores 0.556, because that is the drafter's agreement rate. The trained judge was well below the do-nothing baseline.

Two structural causes, both fixed in round 2a:

  1. The model could not tell a guess from a fact. In round 1's input, a tentative draft token and a permanently committed token are byte-identical. So the judge had to first infer which slots it was even being asked about — impossible when the drafter's mistakes are plausible. At inference we know exactly which slots are drafts, and we were withholding it. Every speculative-decoding verifier is told; every diffusion model is told its noise level. We told it nothing.
  2. The adapter leaked. One delta applied at every position let the judging task bleed into masked and committed slots, disturbing ordinary decoding.

Round 2a adds the flag and the gate, freezes the router gates, keeps the same data and the same 1,057-step budget, and is trained from scratch — so any gain is attributable to the mechanism and not to more compute.

{{FIG_F4}}

The repair is unambiguous at the level of the mechanism. Held-out accuracy at draft slots goes 0.393 → 0.579, finally above the 0.556 copy baseline; confident commits go from 77% right to 96% right; and the all-mask regime stays pinned at the stock value through every evaluation, exactly as the gating guarantees.
