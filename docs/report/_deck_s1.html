Chapter I · the machine

What one pass actually costs, from memory traffic

A mixture-of-experts layer only runs 8 experts per slot, but 32 slots choose independently, so the layer must fetch the weights of their union. That union, not the top-8, is the cost.

bytes per MoE forward = 58 experts × 48 layers × 3 matrices × 768 × 2048 × 2 bytes ≈ 26 GB + ~3 GB dense drafter 1.7B, bf16 = 3.4 GB one drafter forward ≈ 1/8.5 of a 30B pass

So the honest unit is the expert-layer execution: one expert, at one layer, in one pass. Divide by tokens produced and you get a number that does not depend on our kernels.

Chapter I · the machine

Why nine passes: a marginal is flat where a conditional is peaked

The block does not take nine passes because the model is unsure what to say. It takes nine passes because of which question a masked slot is being asked.

A slot whose token is forced by the prefix — the second digit of 10 × 2 = 20 — gets 0.99 and commits at once. Everything phrasable stalls. Each commit forces about three more slots, which commit next pass, which force three more. That chain is the nine passes.

The waste is measurable, and it is a third of the block

The model is not wrong at those slots. It is insufficiently sure of something it already has right, because the marginal is spread over paraphrases.

The whole opportunity in one sentence. If something supplies one concrete completion, a third of the block turns from "flat and stalled" into "peaked and committable" without the model changing its mind about anything.
