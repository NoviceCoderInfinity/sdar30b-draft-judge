The hypothesis: it is under-trained, not wrong

"Train it more" is usually a bad answer. Here there is specific evidence for it, and it is worth being precise about what that evidence is and what it is not.

the case for
  • Nothing had plateaued. Accuracy, confident share and precision were all still climbing at step 1,057, and accuracy was accelerating.
  • Not a learning-rate artefact. The schedule only decayed to half of peak, so it was still learning when the run ended.
  • The corpus was known to be too small beforehand. ~600,000 supervised slots against dParallel's 4–9 million. Round 2b takes it to ~2.3×.
  • The bottleneck is recall, not correctness. Confirmations are already 99% precise. The judge is simply not sure often enough.
What that buys. Confident slots 0.39 → ~0.52 lifts confirmations from 35% to ~43% of open slots: capturing 78% of the available correct drafts instead of 62%. About 3 more commits per pass, at 99% precision.

The two things that would most change my mind

what is missing, and it is our fault
  • The no-draft control has never run. Same recipe, draft disabled, certainty forcing alone. If it matches the draft arm, the draft adds nothing. We cannot rule that out today.
  • The trained judge has never seen 500 items. Every trained number here carries a ±5-point interval. The frozen cell is queued but has not run.
Bluntly. The strongest supportable claim today: a 27 M-parameter adapter teaches a diffusion LLM to read its own filled slots, 38% → 94% end to end, landing level with a rule that costs nothing. Everything past that is a hypothesis with a schedule attached.
what would make me raise the odds
  • Confident share past 0.45 by step 2,000 with precision holding above 0.96. That is on-trend and would mean the recall lever is real rather than extrapolated.
  • Override precision above 0.90 at τ = 0.95. That single number is what currently forces the strict threshold that gives the pass savings back.
  • Drafter agreement past 0.65. It raises the confirmation ceiling for free and makes every later judge round easier.
One cheap thing worth doing regardless. Deploy the draft-informed expert pool. It attacks cost per pass, so it can rescue a partial result: if passes stall at 7 but each touches 32 experts instead of 58, the cost-per-token claim survives on its own.
Chapter VIII · the honest position

What is settled, what is not, and what the paper is either way

settled
  • The mechanism findings. Filled slots return the next input token. Draft context makes the model sycophantic. The empty-block marginal is the only honest free signal.
  • The free rule is held out. +0.2 [−1.4, +1.8] points, 16% fewer passes, 500 unseen items.
  • Flag-and-gate is the right fix. 0.393 → 0.579 and 38% → 94%, at identical data and budget.
not settled
  • Whether the judge can beat the free rule at all. On today's evidence it ties.
  • Whether anything survives 500 items. These are 100-item numbers, ±5-point intervals.
  • The pre-registered gates are not met. Target 1.5 points at ≤5 passes and ≤582 runs. Best: 1 point at 7.93 and 995.
  • The no-draft control never ran. So we cannot separate "the draft helps" from "certainty forcing helps".
