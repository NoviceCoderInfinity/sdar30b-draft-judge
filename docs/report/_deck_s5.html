Round 1 lost to a policy with no parameters at all

The diagnostic is one comparison. There is a trivial policy available to any judge: just copy whatever token is already in the slot. It scores 0.556, because that is the drafter's agreement rate. Round 1 scored 0.393.

The part that stings. At inference we know which slots are drafts — the decoder just placed them there. Every speculative-decoding verifier is told which tokens are speculative; every diffusion model is told its noise level. Round 1 withheld free information and then asked the model to infer it from tokens designed to look plausible.

Round 2a: same data, same 1,057 steps, the flag added

The repair is unambiguous at the level of the mechanism. Same corpus, same budget, trained from scratch — so the gain belongs to the flag and the gate, not to more compute.

  • 0.393 → 0.579 at draft slots, finally above the copy baseline.
  • 77% → 96% precision on confident verdicts.
  • All-mask decoding never moved. Pinned at the stock 0.532 at all ten evaluations — the gate makes that a theorem, not a hope.
  • End to end: 38% → 94%.
And the tell for what is still missing. The judge is right when it is confident, but it is only confident at 39% of slots — and that share was still rising when the cosine schedule decayed to zero. It is under-trained, exactly as the data-budget arithmetic predicted.

The decode loop: commit, keep, or re-mask

One 30B pass per round, with every open slot holding the draft and the flag armed there. Then a three-way verdict per slot, and repeat.

  • Commit to the judge's argmax if it beats τ. Never revisited.
  • Keep tentative — leave the draft token in place as context — if the judge's argmax agrees with it but is not confident enough.
  • Re-mask if the judge's argmax disagrees.
The middle branch is the contribution. A stock decoder has no such state: an uncommitted slot is blank. Here a slot the model half-believes stays readable to its neighbours, so the next pass conditions on it instead of on nothing.
