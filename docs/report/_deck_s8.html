Same objective it was born with. Different teacher.

The drafter is itself a block-diffusion model, built from Qwen3-1.7B with 50 B tokens of continued pretraining and 4 B tokens of supervised fine-tuning, both under the objective below. We do not invent a new one. We change y.

own answer to the same problem, kept only when correct Hard-label sequence distillation: the student trains on the teacher's own samples (Kim & Rush 2016). All 1.7 B parameters move.
Why this is the standard move. Speculative decoding always trains its drafter on the target model's outputs. What is unusual here is only that the target is a diffusion model, so the objective the drafter already has happens to be exactly the right one to reuse.

Enough for what, exactly. Enough to move style: which of several fine phrasings to prefer, how to lay an equation out. That is a low-information change and it is precisely what raises token-level agreement. Not enough to teach new mathematical ability — and it does not need to, because the judge supplies the correctness.

Gate 1 — paired agreement. Replay the teacher's blocks on 100 held-out problems, count slot agreement, fine-tuned against stock, bootstrap interval on the per-item difference. Stock is 0.558. If agreement does not rise, the fine-tune is discarded.
Gate 2 — the drafter's own accuracy. Decode GSM8K with the 1.7B alone, before and after. If that falls by more than a few points while agreement rises, it is copying surface form and losing competence, and we switch to LoRA at a lower rate. This gate is not built yet.

A better drafter also raises the bar it has to clear

Which is why round 3 is not a free win. It lifts the ceiling on confirmations and it lifts the baseline at the same time. The question it answers is not "is the drafter better" but "does the judge extract more from a better drafter than the free rule does".
