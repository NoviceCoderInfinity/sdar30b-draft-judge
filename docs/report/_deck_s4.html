Chapter V · the method

Why attention is the right thing to edit

Both behaviours the judge needs already exist in the network. Nothing new has to be learned from scratch.

  • "Copy the token at my own slot" is an identity attention pattern — that is what fires at filled slots today.
  • "Infer a slot from its neighbours" is the masked-slot machinery SDAR spent 50 B tokens training.
  • The adapter's whole job is to make the second fire where the first currently does. That is a change in where attention looks — exactly what a low-rank update to q, k, v, o expresses.
This is why 27 M parameters is a plausible budget for undoing a habit inherited from trillions of pretraining tokens. We are not teaching a new skill; we are re-routing between two the model already has.

One training example: the drafter's own mistakes as the corruption

An example is one (item, block) pair, and its state is re-randomised every time it is drawn, so the model meets the whole trajectory rather than one snapshot of it.

with prob 0.4 nothing committed (pass-1 state) else a recorded pass's committed set each open slot then holds the draft token w.p. 1 − m [MASK] w.p. m, m ~ U(0,1) with prob 0.3 m = 0 exactly (the literal pass-1 inference state)
The corruption is the real drafter's errors. Published work on correcting filled tokens corrupts with uniform or self-sampled noise. Here the wrong tokens the judge learns to catch are precisely the wrong tokens it will meet at inference.
input only, never a target ▒ [MASK] — the ordinary diffusion regime, kept in the mix The target at every open slot is the token that block ended up with. Draft right → a confirmation. Draft wrong → an override.

The loss: say the right token, and be sure about it

L = meanₖ ∈ ₒ [ −log pᵢ(Tᵢ) ] cross-entropy, every open slot + β · meanₖ ∈ ℂ H( softmax(zᵢ / τ) ) sharpen, ONLY where already right ℂ = { i ∈ ₒ : argmax pᵢ = Tᵢ } β = 2.0 τ = 0.5 [MASK] column removed

AdamW, lr 2e−5, cosine, global batch 32 states, 1,057 steps = two epochs over 16,905 blocks. 5.3 s per step on 8 GPUs.

  • Term one teaches what to say. Cross-entropy against the block's final tokens.
  • Term two attacks the bottleneck. A third of slots are right but under 0.95. Where the top guess is already the target, push it toward 1 so the slot clears the threshold in one pass instead of three.
  • Never applied to a wrong slot. It sharpens a correct distribution; it cannot entrench a wrong one.
  • [MASK] is deleted before the entropy. Otherwise entropy could be minimised by piling probability on a token that is never a legal output.
Honest about scale. ~600,000 supervised slots, 7 to 15× less than dParallel used for a comparable behaviour change. Under-confidence was flagged in advance as the likeliest failure. It is exactly what happened.
