How the trained judge decodes

One forward pass per round, with every open slot holding the draft, and a three-way verdict per slot.

  1. The drafter writes the block in 8 steps → D.
  2. The big model runs one forward pass with every open slot holding D — no masks at all — and the draft flag armed at exactly those slots.
  3. Per slot: commit to the judge's argmax if its probability beats τ; otherwise keep the draft as tentative context if the judge's argmax agrees with it; otherwise re-mask the slot.
  4. Repeat on the new state. At least one commit is forced per pass, so it terminates. Then the cache flush, as always.

Step 3 is the part with no analogue in speculative decoding. A rejected slot does not discard the slots after it; it just goes back to being a question, with everything the pass did settle now available as context.

Where the remaining errors come from

The judge's mistakes are not spread evenly. They live almost entirely in one of its two kinds of decision, and that is what makes the result diagnosable rather than merely disappointing.

Split every confident commit in two. A confirmation is a commit where the judge's argmax equals the drafter's token — it agrees. An override is a commit where the argmax differs — it overrules the draft. On 8,687 held-out pass-1 slots, at τ = 0.95:

Nearly every wrong confident commit is an override. When the judge agrees with the draft it is right 99 times in 100; when it overrules the draft it is wrong 17 times in 100. Raising only the override threshold to 0.99 lifts override precision to 0.953 and cuts expected damage from 0.46 wrong commits per block to 0.14 — which is exactly the asymmetric rule in the results table, and exactly why it recovers accuracy.

But it recovers accuracy by giving the pass savings back, and that is the crux:

Why the judge ties instead of winning

Confirmations are capped and overrides are unreliable. Confirmations can never exceed the drafter's 55.5% agreement, and the judge is confident enough to take only 62% of the correct drafts it sees. The decisions that would break through that ceiling — overrides, where the judge contributes something the drafter could not — are precisely the 17%-wrong ones. Make them safe and they nearly vanish; leave them loose and they cost accuracy.

The failure mode is visible in the generated text. Stock and the free rule never break the answer format. The judge at τ = .95 breaks it in 2 or 3 of its 9 wrong answers: correct reasoning ending in \boxed{180$. with a missing brace, or boxed{64}$ with a missing backslash. A wrong override of a single token in the answer wrapper flips the whole item. Under confirm .90 / override .99 no format failures occur at all — all six wrong answers are genuine reasoning errors.

What is settled, what is not, and what would change it

Settled

  • The mechanism findings. Filled slots on a masked-diffusion model return the next input token; draft context makes the stock model sycophantic; the empty-block marginal is the only honest free signal. These are clean measurements on 15,782 slots and they explain a family of failed approaches.
  • The training-free rule works and is held out. +0.2 accuracy points [−1.4, +1.8] with 16% fewer passes on GSM8K-500.
  • The flag-and-gate design is the right fix for round 1. 0.393 → 0.579 at draft slots, 38% → 94% end to end, at identical data and budget, with normal decoding provably untouched.
  • Draft-informed expert pools cover better than mask-informed ones. A top-32 pool voted from a draft-filled block covers 0.754 of the final tokens' expert picks against 0.511 from masks, paired difference +0.242 [+0.233, +0.252]. This lever has been measured but never yet put in a decode.

Not settled

  • Whether the judge can beat the free rule at all. On present evidence it ties. Every trained point sits on or inside the free-rule frontier.
  • Whether any of this survives 500 items. The trained results are 100-item numbers with ±5-point intervals. A 1-point deficit is not distinguishable from a 3-point one.
  • The pre-registered gates are not met. The target was accuracy within 1.5 points at ≤5 passes per block and ≤582 expert-layer executions per token. Best achieved: 1 point at 7.93 passes and 995 executions.
  • The T0 control never ran. Without the same recipe trained without a draft, we cannot yet separate "the draft helps" from "certainty forcing helps".

The three levers still open

  1. Confirmation recall. The judge's confident share was still climbing when the learning rate decayed (0.35 → 0.39 over the last 400 steps). More data and more steps should convert correct-but-timid confirmations into commits — the cheapest possible win, since confirmations are already 99% precise. running
  2. Override precision. Same route: the overrides are the only decisions that can push past the drafter's ceiling, and they need to get from 0.83 to about 0.95 at τ = .95 rather than at τ = .99. running
  3. The drafter's 55.5% agreement itself. Fine-tune the 1.7B on the big model's own trajectories, which is standard practice for speculative-decoding drafters and directly raises the cap on confirmations. queued

Running right now

Round-2 data generation is on all 8 GPUs: the remaining GSM8K prompts, a third of MATH train, and MBPP. The snapshot at the time of writing is about 38,600 usable blocks, roughly 2.3× round 1. When it finishes, an orchestrator trains the drafter on one GPU and resumes the judge for 2,000 more steps on the other seven, then evaluates four commit rules. Neither round 2b nor the drafter fine-tune has run yet; everything in this section is code that exists, not a result.
